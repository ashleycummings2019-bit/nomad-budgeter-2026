### Krakow, Poland: The Flat Tax Advantage

Poland has long been an attractive destination for IT professionals and freelancers due to its competitive tax regime. For digital nomads establishing tax residency in Poland, the key lies in understanding the available options for entrepreneurs and securing **entrepreneur residency**.

The most common path for freelancers is registering as a sole proprietor (działalność gospodarcza). While standard progressive income tax rates apply, many IT and specific service professionals can opt for a "lump sum tax" (ryczałt od przychodów ewidencjonowanych) with rates as low as **12% or 15%** for certain services, and even **8.5%** for others, on gross revenue. This significantly reduces the tax burden compared to many Western European nations. You'll also need to factor in social security and health insurance contributions, which are generally lower than in many EU countries.

Krakow, as a major tech hub, offers a robust ecosystem for remote workers, making the administrative process relatively straightforward with local legal assistance. For a deeper dive into the specifics, consult our [Krakow Tax Guide](/cities/krakow/).

### Budapest, Hungary: Entrepreneurial Incentives

Hungary, particularly Budapest, has also positioned itself as an attractive location for entrepreneurs and digital nomads. Similar to Poland, the focus is on establishing a local business entity or becoming a sole proprietor to gain **entrepreneur residency**.

While the popular KATA (Közteherviselési Adó egyéni Vállalkozók Egyszerűsített Adója) tax scheme underwent significant reforms in 2022, primarily restricting it to individuals providing services to private citizens, other simplified tax regimes or standard corporate/personal income tax rates still apply. For self-employed individuals, the general personal income tax rate (SZJA) is **15%**, with mandatory social contributions (pension, health, unemployment) on top. However, specific allowances and deductions can make this competitive, especially when structured correctly with local expertise.

Navigating Hungarian tax law requires careful planning, especially with recent changes. It's crucial to consult with a local expert to ensure compliance and optimize your structure for **entrepreneur residency**. ### Digital Nomad Visas & Residency for 2026

Securing legal residency is a critical step for any digital nomad. Both Poland and Hungary offer pathways for long-term stays that cater to remote workers, though their approaches differ.

*   **Poland's Digital Nomad Visa (Remote Work Visa):** Poland has introduced a dedicated **digital nomad visa** (often referred to as a 'remote work visa' or 'entrepreneur visa' depending on the specific pathway chosen) for non-EU/EEA citizens. This visa typically requires proof of stable income from outside Poland, health insurance, and accommodation. It's designed for individuals who want to establish tax residency and contribute to the local economy while working remotely for foreign clients. For those seeking **entrepreneur residency**, registering a sole proprietorship is a common and well-established route.
*   **Hungary's White Card (Fehér Kártya):** Hungary does not have a traditional **digital nomad visa** in the same vein as some other EU countries. However, they offer the "White Card" (Fehér Kártya), a specific residence permit for remote workers. This permit is available to non-EU/EEA citizens who can prove they are employed by a company outside Hungary or own a company outside Hungary and perform their tasks remotely. Key requirements include demonstrating a minimum income (typically around €2,000-€3,000 per month), health insurance, and accommodation. For those looking for a more integrated **entrepreneur residency**, registering as a sole proprietor (egyéni vállalkozó) under the general tax rules is another option, though it involves more local administrative steps.
