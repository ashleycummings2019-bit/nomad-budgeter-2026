## Tbilisi — The Cultural Capital

Tbilisi, Georgia's capital, is a city where ancient history meets modern dynamism. Nestled in a valley, its charming Old Town, sulfur baths, and vibrant arts scene make it a magnet for those seeking a rich cultural experience alongside their digital nomad lifestyle.

### Vibe & Lifestyle

Tbilisi offers a bustling, cosmopolitan atmosphere. Its streets are alive with cafes, wine bars, co-working spaces, and a thriving expat community. The city is a hub for arts, music, and gastronomy, providing endless opportunities for exploration and networking. You'll find a mix of historical architecture and contemporary design, giving it a unique character. For those who thrive on urban energy and cultural immersion, Tbilisi is an undeniable draw.

### Cost of Living

Compared to Western European or North American cities, Tbilisi remains remarkably affordable. Rent, dining out, and transportation are budget-friendly, allowing your 1% taxed income to stretch significantly further. You can enjoy a high quality of life without the exorbitant costs associated with other major cities.

### Infrastructure & Connectivity

As the capital, Tbilisi boasts robust digital infrastructure. High-speed internet is widely available and reliable, crucial for any digital nomad. The public transport system is efficient, and ride-sharing apps are readily available. The city also has a growing number of modern co-working spaces, fostering a productive environment. ### Compliance & Practicalities

Setting up your 1% micro-business in Tbilisi is a well-trodden path. There's a strong ecosystem of English-speaking accountants and lawyers familiar with the process. Banking is relatively straightforward, with several local and international banks catering to expats. The administrative processes are generally efficient, making it a frictionless entry point for establishing your tax residency.

## Batumi — The Black Sea Gem

Batumi, located on Georgia's Black Sea coast, presents a stark contrast to Tbilisi. It's a city of striking modern architecture, palm-lined boulevards, and a relaxed, resort-like atmosphere. While smaller than Tbilisi, Batumi offers a unique appeal for nomads seeking a different pace.

### Vibe & Lifestyle

Batumi is often described as Georgia's "Las Vegas" due to its modern skyscrapers, casinos, and vibrant nightlife, particularly during the summer months. The city's main draw is its proximity to the Black Sea, offering beach access and a more relaxed, vacation-like feel. It's ideal for those who enjoy coastal living, water activities, and a less frenetic pace than a capital city. The community is smaller, but growing, attracting those who prefer a quieter, yet still dynamic, environment.

### Cost of Living

Batumi generally offers a slightly lower cost of living than Tbilisi, particularly for rent outside the peak summer season. Groceries and local services can also be marginally cheaper. This makes it an even more attractive option for maximizing your "Savings Moat" on a 1% tax rate.

### Infrastructure & Connectivity

Batumi's infrastructure is rapidly developing. High-speed internet is available, though perhaps not as ubiquitous as in Tbilisi's most central areas. The city is walkable, and local transport is accessible. While there are fewer dedicated co-working spaces compared to Tbilisi, the number is increasing, reflecting its growing popularity among remote workers.

### Compliance & Practicalities

The process for registering your 1% micro-business is identical to Tbilisi. However, finding English-speaking support for legal and accounting services might require a bit more effort, though it's certainly available. ## Navigating Georgian Compliance: Beyond the 1%

While the 1% tax is a major draw, maintaining compliance is paramount. Remember, in 2026, "Residency is not a feeling. It is a paper trail."

### The 183-Day Reality

Even with Georgia's favorable tax system, you must understand the nuances of tax residency. The 183-day rule is a common misconception. Many countries use "Center of Vital Interests" tests. To ensure you're legally tax resident in Georgia, you must formally register your small business, maintain a local address, and ensure your primary ties are to Georgia. Tax authorities use AI-driven data matching, so simply "saying" you live somewhere isn't enough.

### Small Business Registration

The process to register as an individual entrepreneur (IE) and qualify for the 1% tax status is relatively straightforward. It involves registering with the Public Service Hall, obtaining a tax identification number, and applying for the small business status. This is a crucial step in formalizing your Georgian tax residency.
