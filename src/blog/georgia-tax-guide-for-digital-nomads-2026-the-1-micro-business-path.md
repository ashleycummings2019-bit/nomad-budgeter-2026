## Understanding Georgia's Micro-Business Status

The core of Georgia's appeal lies in its "Small Business Status," often referred to as the "Micro-Business" scheme. This program is specifically designed to attract individual entrepreneurs (IEs) and freelancers who provide services remotely.

### Who Qualifies for the 1%?

To qualify for the preferential 1% tax rate, you must register as an **Individual Entrepreneur (IE)** in Georgia. The primary criteria include:

*   **Income Threshold:** Your annual turnover must not exceed 500,000 Georgian Lari (GEL), which is approximately $185,000 USD (as of 2026, subject to exchange rate fluctuations). If you exceed this, your tax rate increases to 3% for the remainder of the year and 5% the following year.
*   **Activity Scope:** The 1% rate applies to a wide range of activities suitable for digital nomads, including IT services, consulting, graphic design, marketing, and other remote professional services. It's crucial to ensure your specific activity falls within the approved list. Certain activities, like financial services or specific types of consulting, might be excluded or subject to a higher rate.
*   **No Employees:** Generally, this status is for individual entrepreneurs. While you can outsource, having full-time employees can complicate your status.

### The Process: Registering as an Individual Entrepreneur

The path to securing your 1% tax rate in Georgia is remarkably straightforward, but requires methodical execution:

1.  **Establish Residency:** While not strictly a "digital nomad visa," obtaining a Georgian long-term visa or simply entering visa-free (for many nationalities) and then registering your address is the first step. Many nomads opt for a temporary rental agreement to establish an address.
2.  **Open a Local Bank Account:** This is critical for receiving income and demonstrating your economic ties to Georgia. Major banks like Bank of Georgia or TBC Bank are popular choices. For managing international transfers and initial setup, a multi-currency account like [Wise](https://wise.prf.hn/click/camref:1101l5JGeT) is an invaluable tool, allowing you to seamlessly convert funds to GEL and back.
3.  **Register as an Individual Entrepreneur:** This is done at the Public Service Hall. You'll need your passport, a local address, and a Georgian tax number. The process is typically quick, often completed within a day.
4.  **Apply for Small Business Status:** Once registered as an IE, you apply for the "Small Business Status" with the Georgian Revenue Service. This confirms your eligibility for the 1% rate.
5.  **Monthly Declarations:** You'll be required to file monthly declarations of your income and pay the 1% tax. This is generally a simple online process.

While the process is streamlined, retaining a local agent or accountant is highly recommended to ensure compliance and navigate any specific decree updates.

## Compliance & Avoiding Pitfalls

While Georgia's 1% path is clear, global tax compliance remains complex. In 2026, tax authorities are leveraging AI-driven data matching, tracking physical location via flight manifests and credit card data. **Residency is not a feeling. It is a paper trail.**

### The 183-Day Trap Revisited

The common misconception that staying in a country for less than 183 days automatically exempts you from tax residency is **false**. Many countries, particularly high-tax nations like the UK or Spain, employ "Center of Vital Interests" tests. If your family, primary bank accounts, or main "base" remains in your former home country, you could still be deemed a tax resident from day one, regardless of your physical presence elsewhere. To truly break ties, you must formally sever them.

### US Citizens: The FEIE/FBAR/FATCA Reality

For US citizens, the journey to tax optimization is unique due to the US's citizenship-based taxation.

*   **FEIE (Foreign Earned Income Exclusion):** You can exclude approximately $120,000 of earned income from US taxation, but you *must* meet either the Physical Presence Test (330 days abroad) or the Bona Fide Residence Test. Living in Georgia and paying 1% tax does not exempt you from US reporting or the need to qualify for FEIE.
*   **FBAR (Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts Report):** If the aggregate value of your foreign accounts exceeds $10,000 at any point in the year, you must report them to the US Treasury. This includes your Georgian bank accounts.
*   **CFC Rules (Controlled Foreign Corporation) & GILTI:** If you operate a foreign corporation (e.g., a Georgian LLC, though most nomads use IE status), the "Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income" (GILTI) rules might still tax you in the US on certain foreign income. This is less common for individual entrepreneurs but crucial for those considering more complex structures.

### Non-US Nomads: Exit Taxes

Many high-tax countries, including Canada, Australia, and Germany, impose "Exit Taxes" when you formally cease residency. This can mean being taxed on unrealized capital gains (e.g., investments, real estate) the moment you leave. It is imperative to consult with a tax professional in your home country to understand and formally "break ties" to stop the tax clock before moving to Georgia.
