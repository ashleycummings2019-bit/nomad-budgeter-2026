### Accommodation

Medellín offers a wide spectrum of housing options. For budget nomads, shared apartments or rooms in local neighborhoods like Envigado or Sabaneta can be incredibly affordable. Mid-range nomads often opt for private apartments in Laureles or Envigado, while premium options are readily available in El Poblado, offering luxury amenities and prime locations.

For longer stays, consider furnished apartment providers like [Blueground](https://www.theblueground.com/?utm_source=nomadbudgeter&utm_medium=toolkit&utm_campaign=All_2026_BuildMngrAuto) (use promo code **NOMADBUDGETER** for 5% off) for a hassle-free setup, especially when first arriving.

### Food & Dining

Eating like a local is incredibly budget-friendly. A "menú del día" (daily lunch special) can cost as little as $3-5. Groceries from local markets (Plaza Minorista) or major supermarkets (Exito, Jumbo) are also very affordable. Dining out frequently in trendy restaurants in El Poblado will quickly push your food budget into the mid-range or premium categories.

### Transportation

Medellín boasts an excellent, clean, and efficient metro system, which is the most cost-effective way to get around. Taxis and ride-sharing apps like Uber and Didi are also widely available and reasonably priced, especially compared to Western cities.

### Utilities & Internet

Electricity, water, and gas are generally affordable. High-speed fiber optic internet is common and reliable, crucial for any digital nomad. For seamless connectivity from the moment you land, consider activating an [Saily eSIM](https://saily.tp.st/XPRLV5qw) before your flight.

### Health Insurance

Proof of health insurance is a requirement for Colombia's digital nomad visa. While local options exist, many nomads opt for international providers. ### Co-working Space

Medellín has a growing number of co-working spaces, particularly in El Poblado and Laureles, offering amenities like high-speed internet, meeting rooms, and networking opportunities. Many cafes also serve as informal workspaces.

### Entertainment & Social

From salsa lessons to hiking in the surrounding mountains, Medellín offers a rich social life. Nightlife in El Poblado and Envigado can range from budget-friendly bars to upscale clubs. Cultural events and museums are often inexpensive or free.

### Visa & Residency Costs

Colombia offers a specific Digital Nomad Visa (Visa V Nómada Digital) which allows remote workers to stay for up to two years. ## The 183-Day Trap & Colombian Residency

> [!WARNING]
> **Residency is not a feeling. It is a paper trail.**
> In 2026, tax authorities use AI-driven data matching to track your physical location via flight manifests and credit card data. You cannot simply "say" you live somewhere.

While Medellín is a welcoming place, it's crucial to understand Colombia's tax residency rules. Generally, if you spend more than 183 days in Colombia within a 365-day period, you become a tax resident. As a tax resident, you would be liable for Colombian income tax on your worldwide income.

For digital nomads leveraging Medellín for lifestyle arbitrage, the key is often to *not* become a tax resident of Colombia, or to ensure your income is foreign-sourced and structured to minimize local tax liability. This requires careful planning and understanding of both Colombian law and your primary tax residency.

For US citizens, remember the FEIE/FBAR/FATCA reality. Even if you live in Medellín, you still have US tax obligations. Non-US nomads must also consider "Exit Taxes" from their home countries if they haven't formally broken ties. This complexity underscores the need for expert guidance.
