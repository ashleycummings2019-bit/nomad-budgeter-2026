## The Greece 7% Flat Tax: A New Era of European Wealth Architecture

Forget the "Death of the Loophole." Modern tax arbitrage is about **Legislated Programs**, and Greece's 7% flat tax is a shining example. Designed to attract foreign talent and capital, this regime allows eligible individuals to pay a flat 7% income tax on their foreign-sourced income for up to 10 years. This isn't about hiding; it's about choosing a better partner for your financial future.

This program positions Greece as a compelling alternative to traditionally high-tax European nations, allowing you to significantly reduce your tax burden while enjoying a premium lifestyle. It's a strategic move for digital nomads looking to maximize their "Savings Moat" within the EU.

### Who Qualifies for the Greek 7% Tax Regime?

To unlock the benefits of Greece's 7% flat tax, you must meet specific eligibility criteria:

*   **Non-Resident Status**: You must not have been a tax resident of Greece for the previous five out of six years before relocating. This ensures the program targets new arrivals, not existing residents.
*   **Relocation for Tax Purposes**: You must relocate your tax residency to Greece. This implies a genuine intention to establish your primary residence and center of vital interests in the country.
*   **Employment or Business Activity**: You must be employed by a foreign entity, perform independent business activities, or be a freelancer whose income is primarily foreign-sourced.
*   **Tax Cooperation Agreement**: You must be a tax resident of a country with which Greece has an administrative cooperation agreement in tax matters. Most developed nations fall into this category.

Understanding these prerequisites is the first step in architecting your tax-efficient move to Greece.

## Key Considerations for the Greek 7% Tax

While the 7% flat tax is incredibly attractive, a deeper understanding of its nuances is essential for robust wealth architecture.

### The "Center of Vital Interests" and the 183-Day Myth (Revisited)

Most nomads believe that if they stay in a country for less than 183 days, they aren't tax resident. **This is false.** Many countries (like the UK or Spain) use "Center of Vital Interests" tests. If your family, your main bank account, or your "base" is there, you are resident from day one. When moving to Greece, ensure you genuinely sever ties with your previous high-tax jurisdiction to avoid dual residency claims. Your paper trail in Greece must clearly demonstrate it is your new center of vital interests.

### Local vs. Foreign-Sourced Income

It's crucial to understand that the 7% flat tax applies specifically to *foreign-sourced income*. Any income you earn from activities performed *within* Greece will be taxed at standard Greek income tax rates, which can be significantly higher. This distinction is vital for planning your business activities and ensuring compliance.

### Social Security Contributions

Even with the 7% flat tax on income, you will generally be required to make social security contributions in Greece. These contributions are separate from income tax and contribute to your pension and healthcare benefits. Factor these into your overall financial planning.

### US Citizens: Navigating FEIE, FBAR, and GILTI

For US citizens, the journey to tax optimization is unique due to worldwide taxation. Even under Greece's 7% regime:

*   **FEIE**: You can exclude ~$120k of earned income, but you MUST meet either the Physical Presence Test (330 days abroad) or the Bona Fide Residence Test.
*   **FBAR**: If you have more than $10k in foreign accounts, you must report them. No exceptions.
*   **CFC Rules**: If you run a foreign corporation, the "Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income" (GILTI) rules might still tax you in the US.

Always consult with a tax professional specializing in US expat taxation.
