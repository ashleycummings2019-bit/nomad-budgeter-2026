Securing your Croatian Digital Nomad Residence Permit requires careful attention to detail.

*   **Proof of Remote Work:** You must be employed or own a company registered outside of Croatia, performing work remotely.
*   **Sufficient Funds:** You need to demonstrate financial stability. The current requirement is generally around €2,539.36 per month (or €30,472.32 for 12 months) in your bank account, though this can fluctuate.
*   **Health Insurance:** Comprehensive health insurance covering your stay in Croatia is mandatory. This is a non-negotiable requirement for all visa applications.

## Module 5: Compliance and Avoiding the 183-Day Trap

> [!WARNING]
> **Residency is not a feeling. It is a paper trail.**
> In 2026, tax authorities use AI-driven data matching to track your physical location via flight manifests and credit card data. You cannot simply "say" you live in Croatia.

While Croatia's Digital Nomad Visa explicitly grants you 0% tax on foreign income, it's crucial to understand your broader tax obligations, especially concerning your previous home country.

### The 183-Day Myth:
Many nomads mistakenly believe that if they stay in a country for less than 183 days, they aren't tax resident. **This is false.** Many countries (like the UK or Spain) use "Center of Vital Interests" tests. If your family, your main bank account, or your "base" is there, you are resident from day one, regardless of physical presence. When moving to Croatia, you must formally "break ties" with your previous tax residency.

### US Citizens: The FEIE/FBAR/FATCA Reality:
For US citizens, the journey to 0% tax is slightly more complex but still achievable:
*   **FEIE (Foreign Earned Income Exclusion):** You can exclude approximately $120k of earned income, but you MUST meet either the Physical Presence Test (330 days abroad) or the Bona Fide Residence Test. Living in Croatia for a year can help satisfy these.
*   **FBAR (Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts Report):** If you have more than $10k in foreign accounts, you must report them annually. No exceptions.
*   **CFC Rules (Controlled Foreign Corporation):** If you run a foreign corporation, the "Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income" (GILTI) rules might still tax you in the US. Proper entity structuring is key.

### Non-US Nomads: Exit Taxes:
Countries like Canada, Australia, and Germany have "Exit Taxes." You may be taxed on your unrealized capital gains the moment you leave. You must formally "break ties" to stop the tax clock and avoid being deemed a tax resident in your home country while living in Croatia. This often involves notifying tax authorities, closing local bank accounts, and severing other financial ties.

**Evidence is paramount.** Keep every boarding pass, every lease agreement, every utility bill, and every official communication related to your move. This paper trail is your defense against any future tax residency challenges.
