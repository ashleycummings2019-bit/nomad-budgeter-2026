### Establishing Tax Residency (and Breaking Old Ties)

To benefit from Thailand's 15% tax, you must establish clear tax residency there. This typically involves:

1.  **Physical Presence:** Spending a significant portion of the year in Thailand (e.g., 183 days or more, though the LTR visa itself implies long-term intent).
2.  **Visa Compliance:** Holding the appropriate digital nomad or LTR visa.
3.  **Local Registration:** Registering with local authorities and obtaining a Thai tax ID.
4.  **Financial Ties:** Opening a local bank account. A multi-currency account like [Wise](https://wise.prf.hn/click/camref:1101l5JGeT) is excellent for international transfers, but a local Thai bank account is crucial for establishing financial ties and managing day-to-day expenses.

Simultaneously, you must formally "break ties" with your previous tax residency. For non-US nomads, this often involves submitting an "Exit Return" and ensuring you no longer meet the "Center of Vital Interests" test in your former home country. For US citizens, while the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) allows you to exclude ~$120k of earned income, you still need to meet the Physical Presence Test (330 days abroad) or Bona Fide Residence Test, and you must file FBAR if you have more than $10k in foreign accounts.
